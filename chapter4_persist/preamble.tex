\chapter{Learning World Models with Persistent Environment Representations}

\section{Preamble}

This chapter presents \hbox{PERSIST}, a world-modelling framework for generating interactive and coherent experiences within a learned simulator. Existing autoregressive world models typically condition generation on a bounded history of pixel observations. Their spatial memory is therefore constrained by the temporal context window, making it difficult to preserve the geometry and state of occluded or previously visited regions over long interactions. PERSIST instead decomposes simulation into four coupled components: a world-frame model that predicts the evolution of a latent 3D state, a camera model that tracks the agent's viewpoint within that state, a world-to-pixel operator that projects the 3D representation into screen space, and a video generator that conditions on this projection and renders the next RGB frame.

This decomposition makes the 3D state, rather than the recent pixel history, the primary carrier of memory. The camera acts as a spatial query into the 3D state, retrieving the information relevant to the agent's current view. The resulting model exhibits improved spatial memory, geometric consistency, and long-horizon stability relative to approaches based on sliding pixel windows or retrieved key frames. The explicit 3D state also enables new capabilities beyond interactive video prediction, including providing a 3D scene as context and performing 3D-edits to the world mid-generation.

Within the broader thesis narrative, this chapter addresses the first condition of the simulation bottleneck: the requirement that an accurate and interactable simulator already exist before RL agents can be trained. PERSIST intervenes on the simulation bottleneck at the level of the simulator itself, investigating how persistent interactive environments can be learned from recorded trajectories. It therefore represents the final step in the thesis's progression from improving learning within a fixed simulator, to augmenting its scenario distribution, and ultimately towards replacing manually engineered simulation with a learned world model. The publication presenting this work is reproduced without modification in the remainder of the chapter.
